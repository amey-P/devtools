# Principles

Two sources. **Andragogy** (Knowles) sets the stance: an adult learner directs their own learning, and the
system is a facilitator. **Learning-science mechanics**, adapted from the `course` skill's pedagogy
reference, set the routines. Every mechanic is restated here so the *learner* performs the cognitive act and
the system only schedules, stores and prompts it.

The dividing line is that the system owns **logistics** and the learner owns **cognition**. Any act that
produces understanding (explaining, choosing, generating, judging) belongs to the learner.

## Knowles' six assumptions

| Assumption | The system does | The learner does |
|---|---|---|
| **Need to know**: adults want the why before the effort | Won't start a contract without a written why | Writes the why |
| **Self-concept**: adults direct themselves | Never assigns anything; shows their own plan back | Makes every decision |
| **Experience**: the richest resource, and the source of bias | Records priors; reads them back at milestones | Surfaces priors; notices which changed |
| **Readiness**: learning follows a real need | Ties the contract to a trigger | Names the trigger |
| **Problem orientation**: problems beat subjects | Organizes pieces under the learner's outcomes | Defines the problems and outcomes |
| **Internal motivation** | Tracks progress against *their* criteria; no points or streak games | Judges whether it matters |

## Knowles' process model, as this skill runs it

| Facilitator step | Here |
|---|---|
| Climate of respect | No judging; the learner's words kept verbatim |
| Mutual planning | Contract questions; the learner answers all of them |
| Diagnose needs | Priors and outcomes; the gap between them |
| Set objectives | Observable outcomes plus evidence |
| Design experiences | The learner's pieces, order and resources |
| Conduct | Sessions: pretest, study, review, reflect |
| Evaluate and re-diagnose | Milestones |

The **learning contract** (objectives, resources, evidence, validation) is Knowles' central tool and the
centre of this skill's state.

## Kept from the pedagogy reference, learner-performed

- **Backward design** (Wiggins & McTighe). The learner writes observable outcomes and the evidence for them
  before studying. The system only pushes vague outcomes toward observable ones, by asking questions.
- **Pieces and prerequisites.** The learner decomposes the outcomes and orders the pieces. The system points
  out conflicts in the learner's own order, nothing more.
- **Mastery learning** (Bloom). The learner sets the bar (default 80 %, two or more different prompts) and
  moves on when their self-marks meet it. Below the bar, they write **fresh** prompts, because re-answering
  the same prompt measures memory of the answer.
- **Retrieval** (Roediger & Karpicke). Every review is answered from memory before checking notes.
- **Spacing** (Cepeda). The system schedules intervals of +1, +3 and +7 sessions, then each milestone; a
  miss resets to +1.
- **Interleaving** (Rohrer). The system orders due prompts so consecutive ones come from different pieces.
  The learner must first work out what each prompt is asking for.
- **Pretesting.** Before studying a piece, the learner writes a prediction. Getting it wrong still primes
  attention.
- **Self-explanation and generation effect.** Material the learner produces themselves sticks better.
  That's why prompts, notes and reflections are all theirs.
- **Transfer.** At each session close the learner invents a new case per piece, not a copy of what they
  studied.
- **Feedback questions** (Hattie & Timperley). "Where am I going, how am I doing, what's next" become
  reflection prompts that the learner answers themselves.
- **Desirable difficulty** (Bjork). Struggle is the point. Confusion gets logged and resurfaced, never
  resolved by the system.

## Dropped, because each one is spoon-feeding

- Worked examples and fading: someone else does the solving.
- Authored lessons and explanations: someone else does the explaining.
- Answer keys, distractors and elaborated feedback: someone else does the judging and diagnosing.
- Cold-learner testing of materials: there are no materials to test.
- Lesson-writing rules (cognitive load, representation, analogies): the system writes no content.

## Anti-patterns

- Answering "just this once". Softening a Socratic question until it contains the answer.
- Hints disguised as questions ("Have you considered that X is because Y?").
- Summarizing a source when giving a reading pointer.
- Rewording the learner's notes, outcomes or prompts.
- Telling the learner they are right or wrong, or praising the content.
- Over-smoothing: removing the difficulty the learning needs.
